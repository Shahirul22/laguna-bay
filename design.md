# Interior Design & 3D Visualization Brief

## 1. Objective

Design and implement a realistic, practical, interactive 3D interior visualization of my new house based strictly on the provided floor plan.

The floor plan is the **source of truth**.

Do not redesign, resize, move, remove, or invent rooms, walls, doors, windows, structural elements, or other architectural features unless explicitly instructed.

The goal is not simply to create an attractive render. The design should represent a house that could realistically be lived in by a small family, while remaining spacious, easy to clean, visually consistent, comfortable, and practical.

The 3D result should allow the user to walk around/inspect the house and understand how the proposed furniture and interior design would actually fit within the available space.

---

# 2. Overall Design Direction

Use a combination of:

* Modern minimalist
* Warm/cozy modern
* Contemporary upscale / light luxury

The result should **not** feel like an overly expensive showroom or a completely sterile minimalist apartment.

Target feeling:

* Spacious
* Calm
* Comfortable
* Clean
* Warm
* Soft
* Modern
* Slightly premium
* Practical for everyday family living

Maintain a consistent design language throughout the house.

The gaming/work room is the intentional exception and can have a more technological/futuristic appearance.

Avoid excessive decoration, excessive furniture, visual clutter, or designs that look impressive but would be difficult to maintain.

---

# 3. Colour & Material Direction

Primary palette:

* White
* Off-white
* Cream
* Beige
* Light wood
* Dark wood
* Grey
* Black accents

Use these colours consistently rather than assigning completely different themes to each room.

Prefer a balanced mixture of:

* Light neutral surfaces
* Natural wood
* Selected dark accents
* Soft furnishings
* Subtle contrast

Avoid excessive use of black or very dark surfaces because the house should remain visually spacious.

Dark accents should be used selectively for things such as:

* Furniture frames
* Lighting fixtures
* Handles
* Decorative details
* TV framing
* Selected cabinetry elements

---

# 4. Flooring

## Main house

Use light-coloured flooring as the default.

The flooring should visually contribute to the feeling of spaciousness.

## Bathrooms

Use an appropriate darker or grey bathroom floor material with suitable bathroom characteristics.

Prioritize:

* Slip resistance
* Water resistance
* Easy cleaning
* Appropriate visual contrast with the walls

## Third room / gaming room

Use vinyl flooring.

The vinyl should complement the modern technology/workstation aesthetic without making the room feel overly industrial.

---

# 5. Living Room

The living room should prioritize **open space and comfortable circulation**.

Required/desired elements:

* Approximately 65" TV
* 3+2 sofa arrangement
* Coffee table if space allows
* TV cabinet
* TV feature wall/frame
* Appropriate lighting
* Curtains
* Air conditioner
* Practical walking space

For the TV wall, explore a combination of:

* Clean TV mounting
* Subtle wood/slat feature
* Neutral wall treatment
* Low/floating TV cabinet
* Controlled ambient lighting

Do not make the TV wall excessively decorative.

The TV should remain the visual focus without overwhelming the room.

Furniture should be arranged so that the room remains easy to walk through and clean.

Do not force every desired furniture item into the room if the actual floor-plan dimensions make it impractical.

---

# 6. Dining Area

There may not be enough space for a dedicated dining room.

Provide a 6-person dining table wherever the floor plan allows it to work most naturally.

Determine the best position based on:

* Available floor area
* Walking clearance
* Kitchen access
* Living room circulation
* Door/window locations
* Overall visual balance

The dining table should feel integrated into the living/dining area rather than appearing to block circulation.

---

# 7. Kitchen

The kitchen should be practical for **occasional cooking**.

Preferred characteristics:

* Closed kitchen if supported by the existing floor plan
* Built-in cabinetry
* Reasonable amount of upper cabinets
* Good storage capacity
* Clean appearance
* Easy-to-clean surfaces
* Practical work triangle/cooking workflow

Cabinet design:

Use a mixture of:

* Wood
* Cream/off-white
* Dark accents

Let the exact combination be determined based on the floor plan and overall design.

Do not force a particular colour combination if another arrangement produces a more coherent result.

Countertop material and colour should be selected to complement the cabinetry and overall house design.

Avoid unnecessary kitchen decoration.

Prioritize usable countertop and storage space.

---

# 8. Master Bedroom

The master bedroom should feel:

* Cozy
* Calm
* Comfortable
* Slightly upscale
* Easy to maintain

Required/desired furniture:

* King-size bed
* Bedside tables
* Wardrobe
* Dressing table
* Appropriate lighting
* Curtains
* Air conditioner

For the wardrobe, consider all reasonable configurations before selecting the most appropriate one:

* Full-height built-in wardrobe
* Sliding-door wardrobe
* Hinged-door wardrobe
* Glass-door elements
* Concealed/plain doors
* Combination of the above

Select the configuration that best fits:

1. Actual room dimensions
2. Door/window placement
3. Walking clearance
4. Storage requirements
5. Overall design language

Do not choose a wardrobe style simply because it looks good in isolation.

---

# 9. Third Room — Dual PC Work / Gaming Room

This room is a dedicated shared workstation and gaming room for two people.

It must contain:

* 2 separate PC setups
* 2 separate desks
* 2 ergonomic chairs
* 4 monitors total
* 2 PCs
* Shelving/storage
* Gaming-related equipment/decor
* Appropriate lighting
* Air conditioner

The two workstations should be positioned **side by side** where the floor plan permits.

Each person should have their own clearly defined workstation.

The room should have a:

### Modern technology / futuristic aesthetic

rather than an exaggerated "gamer cave".

Use:

* Darker accents
* Indirect lighting
* Controlled RGB
* LED strips where appropriate
* Desk lighting
* Modern furniture
* Clean cable management
* Subtle gaming/technology elements

Avoid excessive RGB everywhere.

The room should still look premium and coherent with the rest of the house.

Most importantly:

**Do not overcrowd the room.**

There must be reasonable:

* Chair movement
* Walking space
* Access to doors/windows
* Space behind chairs
* Access to storage
* General maintenance space

If the desired equipment cannot physically fit comfortably, prioritize usability over decoration.

---

# 10. Other Bedroom

For the remaining bedroom, maintain the same overall house design language.

Do not assume a specific use for the room unless the floor plan or user specifies it.

Keep the room flexible and relatively uncluttered.

Use furniture appropriate for a normal bedroom while preserving useful open space.

Use vinyl flooring only if this is the designated third/gaming room; otherwise follow the main flooring specification.

---

# 11. Bathrooms

There are two bathrooms:

* One common bathroom
* One master bedroom ensuite

Use a coherent bathroom design language between both.

Preferred characteristics:

* Grey/darker floor
* Light neutral walls
* Easy-clean surfaces
* Modern fixtures
* Minimal unnecessary decoration
* Appropriate storage
* Good visual contrast
* Practical circulation

The master bathroom may have slightly more premium detailing while still matching the rest of the house.

---

# 12. Entrance / Foyer

The entrance should remain clean and welcoming.

Include:

* Shoe cabinet
* Practical shoe storage
* Mirror if space allows
* Appropriate entrance lighting

Consider an artificial-grass style flooring/detail at the entrance if it works naturally with the overall design.

However, do not use artificial grass simply as decoration if it makes the entrance look artificial or difficult to clean.

Prioritize:

* Easy cleaning
* Moisture/dirt management
* Practical shoe-changing area
* Visual transition between entrance and main living area

---

# 13. Storage Strategy

Use a **balanced storage approach**.

The house should have enough storage without making every wall a cabinet.

Storage should account for:

* Shoes
* Clothes
* Baby-related general items
* Electronics
* Cleaning equipment
* Household items
* General miscellaneous storage

Avoid conventional large open bookshelves because bookshelves are not a priority.

Instead consider:

* Closed cabinets
* Low storage units
* Built-in concealed storage
* Multi-purpose cabinets
* Under-counter storage
* Wardrobes
* TV cabinet storage
* Utility storage

Prefer closed or visually clean storage for items that would otherwise create clutter.

The final house should be easy to tidy.

---

# 14. Curtains & Windows

Use:

**Sheer + blackout double-layer curtains**

throughout appropriate living/bedroom spaces.

Curtain design should complement the neutral palette.

Avoid overly decorative curtain styles.

Consider:

* Natural fabric appearance
* Neutral colours
* Full-height curtains where appropriate
* Clean curtain tracks/rods
* Good relationship with windows and furniture

---

# 15. Lighting Strategy

Lighting should be designed room-by-room rather than using the same lighting everywhere.

## Living room and kitchen

Use a mixture of:

* Bright functional lighting
* Neutral/clean ambient lighting
* Selected warm accent lighting

Target an atmosphere somewhere between:

* Bright modern home
* Neutral contemporary interior

Use things such as:

* Downlights
* Cove lighting
* Pendant lighting where appropriate
* Floor/table lamps
* Subtle LED accent lighting

Do not overuse decorative lighting.

## Bedrooms

Use warmer, cozy lighting.

Include combinations of:

* Main ceiling lighting
* Bedside lighting
* Warm ambient lighting
* Cove/indirect lighting where appropriate
* Floor/table lamps where useful

The bedroom should feel relaxing at night.

## Foyer

Use warmer, welcoming lighting.

## Gaming room

Use a dedicated gaming/workstation lighting concept.

Use:

* Monitor/desk lighting
* Indirect LED lighting
* Controlled RGB
* Ambient wall/ceiling lighting
* Task lighting

RGB should be controlled and intentional rather than covering every surface.

---

# 16. Air Conditioning

Air conditioning is required in:

* Living room
* Master bedroom
* Other bedroom
* Third/gaming room

Do not simply place AC units on random walls.

**Actively determine the most appropriate indoor-unit positions using the floor plan.**

Consider:

* Room size
* Room geometry
* Door positions
* Window positions
* Bed positions
* Sofa positions
* Workstation positions
* Airflow direction
* Circulation
* Maintenance access
* Avoiding direct cold airflow onto people where reasonably possible

For bedrooms, avoid positioning the AC so that the primary airflow directly hits the sleeping position if another practical location exists.

For the gaming room, avoid direct airflow that interferes with workstation comfort.

For the living room, consider how the airflow covers the usable occupied area rather than simply the centre of the room.

If the floor plan makes the ideal location impossible, explain the trade-off and select the most practical alternative.

---

# 17. Furniture Selection Rules

Furniture should generally be:

* Comfortable
* Slim/light where appropriate
* Clean/simple
* Modern
* Easy to clean
* Proportionate to the room

Prefer furniture with visible floor underneath where appropriate because this helps maintain the spacious appearance.

Do not fill empty space just because space is available.

Empty space is intentional.

Maintain realistic furniture dimensions.

Do not use unrealistically tiny furniture to make a room appear larger.

---

# 18. Realistic Clearance Rules

Treat physical usability as a first-class requirement.

Before finalizing furniture placement, check:

* Door opening clearance
* Walking paths
* Chair movement
* Bed access
* Wardrobe access
* Cabinet doors/drawers
* Refrigerator/kitchen access
* Bathroom access
* Window access
* Cleaning access
* AC maintenance access

Furniture must not:

* Block doors
* Block windows unnecessarily
* Prevent drawers/cabinets from opening
* Create unusable corners
* Make normal walking paths excessively narrow

Where there is a conflict between decoration and usability, prioritize usability.

---

# 19. Budget Awareness

Target interior/furniture budget:

**Approximately RM50,000 total, including furniture.**

The design should therefore be visually achievable within a realistic Malaysian residential interior budget.

Do not design every room as a high-end luxury showroom.

Prioritize spending on areas that have the greatest visual and practical impact:

1. Kitchen cabinetry
2. Living room
3. Master bedroom
4. Work/gaming room
5. Essential storage
6. Lighting
7. Curtains
8. Remaining rooms

Use visual techniques such as:

* Material consistency
* Accent walls
* Lighting
* Wood elements
* Furniture proportions
* Controlled decoration

to create a premium appearance without assuming unlimited budget.

The RM50k figure is a design constraint, not a requirement to produce a detailed quotation unless explicitly requested.

---

# 20. Family / Lifestyle Considerations

This is a real family home.

The design should support:

* Two adults
* A young family
* Future day-to-day family use
* Easy cleaning
* Reasonable storage
* Comfortable circulation
* Long-term usability

Do not add baby furniture or baby equipment to the 3D design at this stage.

However, do leave enough flexible space that the house does not become difficult to adapt later.

---

# 21. Visual Consistency

The house should feel like **one house**, not a collection of unrelated Pinterest rooms.

Maintain consistency in:

* Wood tones
* Neutral colours
* Hardware
* Lighting temperature
* Cabinet design
* Furniture language
* Flooring
* Curtain colours
* Accent colours

The gaming room can intentionally diverge in lighting and accent treatment, but it should still feel like part of the same house.

---

# 22. 3D Implementation Requirements

When implementing the design:

1. Start from the provided floor plan.
2. Identify all rooms and their actual boundaries.
3. Preserve the floor plan geometry.
4. Identify doors and windows.
5. Identify usable wall surfaces.
6. Determine realistic furniture dimensions.
7. Place furniture according to the requirements above.
8. Check physical clearance.
9. Determine AC positions.
10. Determine lighting positions.
11. Apply materials.
12. Add decorative elements only after functional requirements are satisfied.

The 3D model should prioritize **spatial accuracy over visual exaggeration**.

Where dimensions are known, use them.

Where dimensions are not known, make reasonable assumptions and clearly identify them rather than pretending they are exact.

---

# 23. Design Decision Process

Do not blindly follow every item in this specification if doing so creates a physical conflict.

For every room:

### First priority

Existing floor plan and physical constraints.

### Second priority

Function and usability.

### Third priority

Circulation and clearance.

### Fourth priority

Furniture requirements.

### Fifth priority

Visual design.

### Sixth priority

Decorative details.

If something cannot fit, do not force it into the room.

Instead:

1. Identify the conflict.
2. Consider alternatives.
3. Select the most practical solution.
4. Document the decision.

---

# 24. Things to Avoid

Avoid:

* Excessive furniture
* Excessive decoration
* Excessive RGB
* Excessive black surfaces
* Extremely dark rooms
* Fake-looking luxury materials
* Unnecessary feature walls everywhere
* Giant furniture that overwhelms rooms
* Tiny furniture that makes rooms look artificially large
* Furniture blocking circulation
* Furniture blocking doors
* Furniture blocking windows unnecessarily
* Unrealistic AC placement
* Decorative items that make cleaning difficult
* Open shelving that creates visual clutter
* Unnecessary bookshelves
* Different interior themes in every room
* Designs that look good only from one camera angle

The final result should look like a **realistic house someone could actually live in**, not just an architectural visualization.

---

# 25. Expected Output

Before implementing major changes, analyze the floor plan and produce a room-by-room design proposal.

For each room, identify:

* Intended purpose
* Furniture
* Furniture placement
* Flooring
* Wall treatment
* Colour palette
* Lighting
* Storage
* AC position
* Major design features
* Potential spatial conflicts
* Assumptions

Then implement the design in the 3D web view.

When making design decisions that depend on the floor-plan dimensions, explain the reasoning briefly.

The final result should balance:

**Spaciousness + comfort + practicality + easy maintenance + consistent modern design.**

The house should feel calm and soft overall, with the gaming room providing the stronger technological contrast.
